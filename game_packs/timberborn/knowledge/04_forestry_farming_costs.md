# Timberborn Forestry, Farming and Building Costs (v0.6)

## Forestry

The Forester building plants trees automatically within its designated zone.
Without a Forester, the colony will eventually exhaust all natural trees and
run out of logs permanently.

Build the first Forester by cycle 2 at the latest. Assign 1–2 beavers.
The Forester zone should cover a large area — trees take several cycles to
mature so plan ahead.

Birch trees: mature in approximately 4–6 days. Medium log yield per tree.
Pine trees: mature faster (approximately 3–4 days) but lower log yield.
Old-growth trees on the map at game start yield more logs when cut — prioritise
cutting old-growth first while your planted forest matures.

Ratio guideline: for every 2 Lumberjack Flags actively cutting, maintain
at least 1 Forester zone replanting. Without this ratio, forests deplete
faster than they regrow.

## Farming

Farmhouses house Farmer beavers who tend crop fields. Each farmhouse
supports a radius of farm tiles.

Crop calorie densities (approximate, per harvest cycle):
- Blueberry: low — good only for early game or emergency food
- Carrot: medium — reliable mid-game staple, fast growth
- Potato: medium-high — slower growth but high yield per tile
- Wheat: low raw calories, but processed into Bread (highest density)

Farm tiles require either natural water adjacency (within ~2 tiles of water)
or irrigation coverage (Irrigation Tower within range).

Do not plant farms on tiles outside water or irrigation range — they will
fail to grow and waste farmer time.

Farmhouse staffing: 1–2 beavers per farmhouse. More beavers do not increase
yield — they reduce harvest lag when crops ripen.

## Population Growth

Beavers reproduce in Breeding Pods. Each pod produces one kit per cycle when
staffed and when colony happiness is sufficient.

Happiness (wellness) is affected by: food variety, entertainment buildings
(stage, campfire, monument), and adequate housing.

Growth bottleneck: planks. Almost every expansion building requires planks.
Never run planks to zero — have at least 10–20 in reserve for emergencies
(a dam breach or flood can destroy buildings requiring immediate replacement).
