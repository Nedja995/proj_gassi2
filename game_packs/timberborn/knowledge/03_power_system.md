# Timberborn Power System (v0.6)

## Overview

Power is required for mechanical buildings: sawmill, water pump (powered
variant), mechanist hut, metal extractor, and others. Power is produced by
Windmills and Water Wheels and stored in Mechanical Batteries.

If a building has no power it halts production entirely. A power shortage
is often invisible until production suddenly stops — check the power network
overlay regularly.

## Windmills

Windmills generate power from wind. Wind strength varies by map elevation:
higher terrain receives stronger and more consistent wind.

Place windmills on the highest available terrain for best output. A windmill
at low elevation may generate 30–50% less power than the same windmill on a
hilltop.

Each windmill generates approximately 60–120 power units depending on wind
conditions and height. Do not rely on a single windmill for critical production.

Windmills produce power during drought seasons as well as wet seasons —
they are the most reliable power source through droughts.

## Water Wheels

Water Wheels generate power from flowing water. They require a tile with
water current — still water produces no power.

Output depends on flow speed: fast-flowing tiles (near dams or narrow
channels) produce more power than slow or wide river sections.

Water Wheels stop producing during drought when the river level drops
and flow ceases. Never rely on Water Wheels as the sole power source —
they are a supplement, not a replacement for windmills.

## Mechanical Batteries

Mechanical Batteries store excess power produced by windmills and water
wheels. They discharge automatically when demand exceeds current production.

Battery capacity: one battery stores approximately 3,600 power units.
Build batteries near your main power consumers to minimise line loss.

Place batteries on high terrain where possible — they can be connected via
power shafts running downhill, and co-locating them with windmills on
hilltops simplifies the network.

Build at least 2 batteries before mid-game. By cycle 8, aim for 4–6 batteries
to buffer overnight and drought periods when water wheel output drops to zero.

## Power Shafts and Distribution

Power travels through Power Shafts (vertical poles). Shafts can run across
terrain but each connection point costs production capacity due to friction
losses in long chains.

Keep power shaft runs short where possible. For long-distance distribution,
build a sub-station windmill on an intermediate high point rather than
running a single shaft across the entire map.

## Signs of Power Shortage

- Buildings showing yellow/orange "no power" icon
- Sawmill stops producing planks despite having logs in stock
- Water pumps stop despite river having water

When a power shortage occurs: check if any windmill is paused or obstructed,
add a battery to handle peak demand spikes, or add another windmill on higher
terrain.
