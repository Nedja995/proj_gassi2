# Timberborn Water Management (v0.6)

## Drought Cycle Mechanics

Drought severity increases with every cycle. Early droughts last 3–5 days.
By cycle 6–8 droughts routinely last 8–12 days. By cycle 12+ droughts can
exceed 20 days.

The drought countdown timer is visible in the top-right HUD. Check it every
in-game day during the wet season — it tells you how many days remain before
the next drought.

Rule of thumb: for every 1 day of upcoming drought you need approximately
500–800 water units in reserve per 10 beavers. At 30 beavers facing a 10-day
drought, target 1,500–2,500 stored water minimum.

## Dams vs Floodgates

Dams are solid barriers. They raise the water level upstream but give no
control — once the water rises above dam height it spills over and floods
downstream land.

Floodgates are the preferred tool. They have a height slider: set the gate
opening height to control exactly how high the water rises behind the dam.
Lower the gate to drain excess water; raise it to store more. Floodgates
prevent accidental colony flooding.

Use floodgates instead of basic dams in almost every situation. The only
exception is a purely decorative barrier with no settlement downstream.

## Water Pumps

Water pumps extract water from rivers or flooded tiles and deliver it to the
supply network (wells, tanks, irrigation).

One water pump typically supplies 8–12 beavers under normal conditions.
During drought, pumps draw from stored water behind dams — they stop
producing when the water level behind them drops to zero.

Place water pumps as deep as possible in the water body to maximise the
drawdown time before they run dry.

## Water Tanks

Water Tank capacity: approximately 3,500 units per tank.

Build at least 2–3 Water Tanks before cycle 3. By cycle 6, aim for 5–8 tanks.
Tanks fill passively when connected to the water network and the pump has supply.

Tanks are the primary drought survival buffer. Each additional tank buys
approximately 1–2 extra drought days for a colony of 20–30 beavers.

## Levees

Levees are one-tile water barriers. Use them to direct water flow, create
channels, and protect buildings from seasonal floods. Levees do not hold
back large volumes — they are flow directors, not storage dams.

## Badwater (Late Game)

Badwater is toxic water that appears during the Badwater Season in later
cycles. It contaminates clean water sources and harms beavers.

To manage badwater: build a completely separate dam and canal network that
captures badwater flow before it reaches your main water supply. Use a
dedicated badwater pump if needed. Never connect badwater canals to your
clean water network.

Badwater taint spreads downstream — isolate it upstream with floodgates
set to block flow from the badwater source during badwater season.

The Detoxifier building (late-game tech) can neutralise badwater into clean
water, but requires significant power and staffing.

## Irrigation

Irrigation Towers and Irrigation Canals extend water access to farm tiles
beyond the natural river reach.

Each Irrigation Tower covers approximately 7×7 tiles. Tiles within range
become "moist" and can support crops without natural water adjacency.

Irrigation is essential for mid-game farm expansion — without it you are
constrained to farming only tiles adjacent to the natural river.

Irrigation canals can chain water from the river to inland irrigation towers.
Plan canal routes before placing farms to avoid blocked paths.
